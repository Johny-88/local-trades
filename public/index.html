Questions

What homeowners usually want to know.

A few clear answers before you post your job.

Is it free to post my job?
Yes. It is free for homeowners to post a job and receive quotes. There is no charge for submitting your request.
What happens after I describe my job?
Your job details are matched with relevant local tradespeople. Interested tradespeople can then respond, giving you options to compare before you decide who — if anyone — you want to hire.
How many quotes can I receive?
You can receive up to three quotes from local tradespeople for the same job, so you can compare your options without having to contact lots of people individually.
Do I have to hire one of the tradespeople?
No. There is no obligation to hire anyone. You can compare the quotes, profiles and customer feedback, ask any questions you need to, and only go ahead if you are comfortable.
Can I trust the customer reviews?
The review system is tied to real jobs. Only the homeowner connected to that completed job can review the tradesperson they hired, and the review is checked by customer service before publication. It is not an open review box where anyone can simply leave feedback.
Get better responses

A clearer job usually gets better quotes.

You do not need to write an essay. A few useful details help tradespeople understand the job properly before they respond.

01

Explain what needs doing

Say what is wrong, what you want changed, and roughly how much work is involved.

02

Add useful photos

For many jobs, a clear photo can answer questions that would otherwise take several messages.

03

Mention timing

Say whether the job is urgent, flexible, or needs to happen before a particular date.

04

Include practical details

Access, parking, measurements, existing materials or anything unusual can help make quotes more accurate.

Before you hire

Five quick checks before you say yes.

A good quote is not just the cheapest number. Make sure you understand what is included and that you are comfortable with the person doing the work.

Compare like for like.If one quote looks much cheaper, check whether labour, materials, waste removal, VAT or finishing work has been left out before assuming it is the better deal.
1
Confirm exactly what is includedAgree the scope of work, materials and any exclusions so both sides know what the quote covers.
2
Check relevant qualificationsFor regulated or specialist work, confirm the registrations or qualifications that apply to that particular job.
3
Ask about timingConfirm when they can start, roughly how long the work should take, and whether anything could delay it.
4
Agree how payment worksUnderstand deposits, staged payments and when the final balance is due before the work starts.
5
Keep the agreement clearFor larger jobs, keep the agreed price, scope and important changes in writing so there is less room for misunderstanding.
